## What is ours and what is not

**This work does not discover that conformal risk control cannot certify extraction. That result is
[arXiv 2606.29054]'s, it is published, and what we measured is substantially a replication of it.**
Their paper establishes the impossibility bound, the base-risk feasibility test, and infeasibility
across multiple systems. We reproduced all three, in a different corpus and a different task.

**The paper was not discovered late.** It was read on 2026-07-18 and its impossibility bound and
base-risk feasibility test were folded directly into the Plan 3 design — three days before ADR 0007
froze the parameters. What was *not* recognised until an adversarial review on 2026-07-22 is that
the resulting negative result was substantially a replication rather than a new finding, and that
the paper's 0.48–0.55 base-risk band had been wrongly attributed in this repository to
document-level extraction when it is in fact two NER datasets and one MMLU subset. Both errors are
ours and both are on the record.

Stating this first is deliberate. A reader who meets our null before that disclosure will reasonably
infer a priority claim we are not making.

Two things here are not in that work, and they are the contribution:

1. **Grounding ⊥ certifiability.** 2606.29054 evaluates no event-argument extraction and does not
   separate grounding from correctness. Measuring that separation is the finding.
2. **The α-frontier as a ruling-out object** — publishing the entire risk–coverage curve so that no
   reader has to take on trust that we did not choose the one operating point where the method fails.

One boundary on the comparison: our measured base risk (QA group loss 0.6071,
`evidence/plan3/certification_result.json`) is **not** a reproduction of their extraction figure and
does not contradict it. Their one extraction task, JSON-Extract, sits at μ = 0.033 — a different
task under a different metric. The comparison marks out a harder operating regime; it settles
nothing about their number.

## 1. Setup

Selective extraction: a system may **abstain** on a document rather than emit a frame. The question
is whether an abstention policy can certify a bounded error rate on the documents it accepts, while
still accepting enough of them to be useful.

- **Corpus:** RAMS, in-domain. Source documents are the independent unit. The sealed certification
  split was opened exactly once, over 3,152 documents, with a verified hash-chained access record
  and zero reads of the `final` or `shift` splits (`CX-SEALED-01`); those 3,152 documents form
  **1,396 groups** (`evidence/plan3/certification_result.json`, and `CX-H1-PRIMARY`'s scope).
- **Systems:** zero-shot (ZS), few-shot (FS), and a QLoRA-adapted model (QA), all served on one
  frozen vLLM 4-bit stack.
- **Target, frozen before any sealed label was opened:** risk α = 0.20, confidence δ = 0.05,
  acceptance floor 0.20.
- **Certificate:** a joint finite-sample SCoRC construction, with a conservative Bonferroni audit as
  a descriptive cross-check.

The target is a **deployment threshold this project committed to, not a natural constant**. Nothing
below relaxes it, and no certificate is issued at any other α.

## 2. The null

**No system certifies** (`CX-H1-PRIMARY`, `CX-H2-SECONDARY`). All three are Infeasible; H1 and H2
collapse to **one** finding, not two, and no numerical difference is computed against an infeasible
baseline.

Under the primary SCoRC construction QA accepts **zero** groups. The conservative Bonferroni audit
agrees that all three are infeasible, and it is in that audit — not under the primary certificate —
that QA came closest to the floor: 296 groups accepted, acceptance lower bound 0.1825 against the
0.20 floor, with a risk upper bound of 0.5157 against α = 0.20
(`certification_result.json`, `systems.qa.conservative_audit`).

**The failure is capability, not method** (`CX-CAPGAP-01`). With an unlimited threshold grid, no
finite-sample penalty and oracle threshold selection, QA could accept **at most 2.0%** of documents
at 20% empirical error. The floor demands 20%. The gap is an order of magnitude, and it is not
caused by the coarse grid, the δ split, the bound looseness, or a split difference — the diagnostic
is pre-bound and carries no δ at all. The same diagnostic on the *development* split gives 3.0%, so
the conclusion reproduces out of sample.

## 3. The finding: a large, real gain in the wrong property

QLoRA produced a substantial improvement in grounding fidelity: **95.7%** of QA outputs parse and
ground to the source document, against **39.4%** ZS and **68.1%** FS, with ungrounded spans falling
from 5,355 to 340 (`CX-GROUNDING-01`). "Valid" here means parseable and grounded, **not correct** —
a span can point at real document text and name the wrong entity or role — and this improvement did
not translate into a certified selective guarantee. Neither half of that sentence may be reported
without the other.

Until this analysis, the gap between those two facts was *interpreted*. It is now **measured**
(`CX-GROUNDING-ORTHOGONAL-01`). Document loss decomposes into four channels, and the group-level
contributions sum **exactly** to the quantity the certificate bounds — so this decomposes the
certified object, not a proxy for it.

| Certification split | QA | FS | ZS |
|---|---|---|---|
| structural failure (forced loss 1.0) | 0.7% | 0.4% | 2.3% |
| grounding failure (forced loss 1.0) | 4.1% | 32.3% | 58.0% |
| valid but contentless | 4.9% | 31.4% | 0.5% |
| **valid and content-bearing** | **90.3%** | **35.9%** | **39.3%** |
| mean loss *within* that channel | **0.579** | 0.947 | 0.986 |

The ~16× separation in ungrounded spans lives in the first three rows. **The certificate scores the
fourth.** 87% of QA's residual loss sits in output that is well-formed, grounded and content-bearing,
and is scored wrong anyway.

**The decomposition locates that loss by channel; it does not identify the error category that
produced it, and this draft claims none.** An earlier revision said the residual was output "that
simply names the wrong entity". That attribution rested on an unquantified inspection of two
development examples, which §12.3 of the accepted plan forbids generalising from, and it was
withdrawn on independent review. A measurement points the other way: on these frames false positives
exceed false negatives by 637 arguments (4,434 against 3,797), which is systematic over-generation
rather than entity-naming, and wrong-role, boundary and coreference errors share that signature. The
machine-coded taxonomy that would separate them (§12.1) is scheduled and has not yet run.

### The counterfactual, with its restrictions attached

Under a **grounding oracle** — each system re-scored as though it never emitted a structural
failure, an ungrounded span or an empty frame, with coverage measured against the restricted
population — max acceptance at α = 0.20 moves from 0.0201 to 0.0374 for QA, and from 0.0000 to
0.0000 for both baselines.

Three restrictions travel with those figures and are not separable from them. **Conditional
coverage under the grounding oracle is not a deployable acceptance rate.** It is **not comparable
across systems**, because the restricted population differs per system; it supports only the
within-system question *"does closing this system's grounding gap reach the floor?"*. And the
decomposition and the counterfactual **select no threshold and certify nothing** — neither is a
certificate, and neither may be presented as one.

**The baselines' result does not rest on that counterfactual, and must not be argued as though it
did.** The restriction is not uniformly generous — dropping a low-loss member raises a group's mean,
and 24 FS and 21 QA groups score *worse* under it. The "0.0000 → 0.0000" holds **under this
construction**, and is not a property of grounding counterfactuals in general: a **correctness
oracle** moves the baselines off zero.

What carries the claim is arithmetic. Accepting a fraction `c` at risk `α` requires population mean
loss ≤ `α·c + (1−c)` = **0.84** at `c = α = 0.20`. ZS (0.984) and FS (0.951) exceed that ceiling on
their restricted populations, so **no selection rule of any kind** reaches the floor for them. QA
sits at 0.582, below the ceiling — so for QA the ceiling is silent and only the measured curve
leaves it short.

The adapter's principal learned behaviour is grounding fidelity, and that is **orthogonal to the
property the certificate measures**, on this corpus and this stack. The grounding number alone is
not the finding; the orthogonality is.

## 4. Ruling α values out, rather than defending one

The obvious objection to a null at a single operating point is that the operating point was chosen.
So we publish the whole curve rather than defend the choice (`CX-ALPHA-FRONTIER-01`).

The oracle risk–coverage curve carries no α: it is one fixed object, and "max acceptance within α"
is that object read at height α. Reading it at every height introduces no new statistic and selects
no threshold.

The 20% floor first becomes oracle-*reachable* at α ≈ 0.406 for QA, 0.930 for FS and 0.981 for ZS —
so the entire range below 0.406 is excluded **without a certificate being computed anywhere in it**.
**This is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one, and it must never be read as an α that would
work.** The curve carries no finite-sample penalty, no grid and no multiplicity correction, and a
certificate at α ≈ 0.406 would still not issue: at the ~280 groups a 20% acceptance implies, the
Hoeffding radius alone is 0.086 at the per-system allocation δ/3 and 0.103 under the family-wide
Bonferroni δ/(2·9), putting the risk upper bound at roughly 0.49–0.51 — over the target it is being
measured against, before any other bound applies. **The curve rules operating points out. It never
rules one in**, and no claim here uses it in that direction.

A planned second arm on WikiEvents was **withdrawn, not deferred** (`CX-WIKIEVENTS-NCERT-01`): the
method needs 1,386 groups and the entire corpus has 246 documents. It is impossible at any α, for
any budget.

## 5. What makes a negative result checkable

**This section is an unledgered case study of one project — no control, no counterfactual, and no
comparative claim about practice elsewhere.** It is reported because the mechanisms are inspectable
in the repository, not because we can show they were necessary. **A block issued against this very
document is not evidence that the apparatus works**: this section would have been written the same
way had none been issued, so no verdict on it could have counted against its own thesis.

- **A claims ledger.** Every public sentence maps to a claim ID carrying its evidence, method, scope,
  limitations and the surfaces it may appear on. A test fails prose citing a claim on an ungranted
  surface — **currently over the README only; this draft is outside it**, so every claim ID here was
  checked by hand.
- **Sealed labels guarded, not merely logged.** Only two roles are reachable from the analysis
  commands; every other raises before a file is opened — replacing a design where an access log
  recorded what a caller *did*, which is worthless without a guard on what a caller *may do*.
- **An independent reviewer.** Eleven committed reviewer records (`evidence/*/reviewer_record*.json`,
  excluding one template; measured at `62b5375`) span roughly ten episodes and carry **four explicit
  BLOCK verdicts** — three in the records, Gate 0F's in `docs/progress.md` — plus a "REVISE" and a
  "NOT YET" that were blocking in effect if not in vocabulary.

None of the four was on the science. A **paid GPU run** was stopped on a bit-exact generated-token
mismatch across two independently quantized loads of the same base model, and exact equality was
kept as a hard gate rather than relaxed to a tolerance. A **cost claim** understated spend against a
fixed ceiling. **This document's first revision** was blocked for miscounting the very reviews
described here, for a design-time claim a committed document contradicts, and for two
claim-boundary breaches — a cross-system comparison the boundaries forbid by name, and a qualifier
a previous review had required.

The fourth is the one worth the space. The **α-frontier re-analysis** was blocked over a path by
which the analysis commands could have opened the sealed `final` split while the artifact they
emitted attested `final_reads: 0` — a disclosure that would have been a lie produced by the
disclosure mechanism itself.

At one discharge the reviewer **recorded a defect against its own contribution**: wording it had
supplied, and the author had adopted verbatim, turned out to be a false universal. The load-bearing
figures in §§2–4 were reproduced by someone who did not compute them — zero mismatches across 100+
independently recomputed values (`evidence/plan3/reviewer_record.reanalysis.json`), grounding counts
reconciled against an independent measurement (`evidence/plan2/reviewer_record.plan2-close.json`).

## 6. Limitations

Beyond those carried per-claim in the appendix:

- **The certificate bounds average within-group loss, not the absence of error** in any document.
  Certified ≠ safe.
- **Scoped to one frozen serving stack** — a probe measured 86.3% parsed-output agreement against
  the training stack (`CX-SERVING-01`, whose limitations include that no threshold was pre-registered
  for it, so the figure is reported rather than judged as a pass, and that it was measured on
  train-split frames only). Portability is not established.
- **In-domain RAMS only**, under span-exact matching.
- **A single seed**, no training ablations.
- **The infeasibility was knowable for $0 before the sealed split was opened**
  (`CX-FEASIBILITY-LESSON-01`) — the development-split diagnostic already showed 3.0% against a 20%
  floor, and neither implementer nor reviewer ran it. Recorded as a joint process failure.
- `final` and `shift` are **descoped and remain sealed**; no shift claim is made in either direction.
